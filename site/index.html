Draw-to-Solve Physics Puzzle

Nature Seed

Released 18 September 2026 Main genre: Draw-to-Solve Physics Puzzle

Nature Seed is a mobile draw-to-solve physics puzzle where you spend a limited line budget to bring Water Drop and Seed together. It launched on 18 September 2026 with 100 levels, efficiency stars, saved progress and watercolor restoration. Play it free in your browser.

Gameplay hook: every committed stroke becomes part of the physical level, and a one-line solve earns three stars.

Casual Merge Puzzle

Candy Shop

Active development Main genre: Casual Merge Puzzle

Candy Shop is a cozy mobile physics merge puzzle. Merge matching candies up a chain of 13 stages, with ten containers that change how the stack behaves and eight special effects. You can hold a piece, preview where it lands and see the next three candies.

Gameplay hook: a familiar drop becomes a new puzzle when the container changes gravity, geometry or the route into the stack.

Physics Puzzle / Arcade Hybrid

Paint Blasters

Production prototype Main genre: Physics Puzzle / Arcade Hybrid

Paint Blasters is a mobile color-mixing physics puzzle about knocking down towers. Combine three colors into one of 35 projectile recipes, then aim at seven types of block and try to turn one exact hit into a chain reaction that brings the tower down.

Gameplay hook: you choose the recipe before you fire, so the mix matters as much as the aim.

Vertical Descent Arcade

Ball is God?!

Active production Main genre: Hardcore Arcade

Ball is God?! is a hardcore vertical descent arcade where a horizontal drag rotates the tower around the falling Espa. Skip three landings to charge a smash, use a cooldown shield against a lethal hit and let karma change enemies, bosses and story choices across ten locations.

Gameplay hook: landing safely is the easy choice, but skipping three platforms builds a smash.

Studio principles

How we design our games

Each game starts from one touch input: draw, drop, mix or rotate. We then add characters, feedback and rules, and cut whatever makes the game hard to follow.

Instant response

Every tap, line, drop and shot gets an immediate reaction on screen, so cause and effect stay clear.

Goals you can see

Motion, contrast and pacing show the goal before any text does. In Nature Seed, a missed line shows what to change on the next try.

A mascot for each game

Each game has a character you recognize at a glance: Water Drop and Seed, a teddy bear, an armored robot and Espa.

Made for phones

Controls and layouts are made for thumbs, short sessions and small screens.

Clear outcomes

Merges, hits, splashes, wins and fails each look different, so you know what just happened.

Relaxed, with room to improve

Nature Seed and Candy Shop look soft but still give you something to master: a one-line three-star solve in the first, a next-three queue in the second.

2D Art Direction

Simple shapes, strong silhouettes and consistent interface art keep each world recognizable.

Living Worlds

Backgrounds, UI and characters respond to what you do, even outside the core gameplay.

What We Build

What is inside the four flagship games.

Physics-based puzzles

Nature Seed turns each drawn line into collision geometry. Paint Blasters lets one exact hit start a tower-wide chain reaction.

Cozy mobile games

Short sessions in soft worlds: calm levels in Nature Seed, repeatable runs in Candy Shop.

Interactive characters

Ball is God?! has 25 characters with dialogue portraits that change expression.

Color-combination mechanics

Three color inputs become one of 35 recipes in Paint Blasters: 5 pure, 20 mixed and 10 rainbow.

Living backgrounds

Nature Seed restores each sketched level to color when you solve it. Ball is God?! descends through ten location scenes.

Containers that change the rules

Candy Shop has ten containers that change gravity, geometry or obstacles, from a flask to rotating gravity.

Progress and collections

Nature Seed keeps unlocked levels and results between sessions. Candy Shop adds a daily challenge, a Candy Book and local top-five scores.

Game UI/UX

Candy Shop's menu, level selection, HUD and command icons share one visual language. Ball is God?! adds dialogue, choice and result screens.

Process

How we make a mobile game.

Idea

Pick a premise and one core action. Nature Seed began as a calm puzzle about connection, nature and restoration.

Prototype

Build the smallest playable version, with real controls, feedback and a way to fail.

Tuning

Adjust controls and pacing until the goal is clear. Nature Seed dropped extra mechanics that made it feel overloaded.

Art Direction

Decide on silhouettes, palette, interface style, icons and characters.

Production

Scale content and systems in Unity. Candy Shop grew into ten container rulesets and a 13-candy chain.

Polish

Tighten effects, menus and physics. Candy Shop had a menu and HUD pass, Paint Blasters an effect stability pass.

Launch

Release a playable build with public press assets. Nature Seed launched free in the browser.

After launch

Read player feedback, publish dated devlog notes and decide what each game needs next.

Privacy

WillowinWorlds privacy note.

The public site is intentionally simple: no analytics scripts, advertising pixels, tracking cookies, third-party embeds or account systems.

Links to social platforms open third-party sites with their own privacy policies; this site does not load social media widgets.

The site may save a small local theme preference in your browser so the day/night switch stays consistent. It does not create a player account or behavioral profile.

The homepage contact form keeps its name, email, inquiry type and message fields in same-tab session storage so a reload does not erase your draft. You can clear it in the form, and the browser removes it when that tab session ends.

If you email WillowinWorlds, your message, email address and links are used only to reply to your inquiry.
